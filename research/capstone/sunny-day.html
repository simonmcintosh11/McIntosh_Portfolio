How the sample day is built

  • Household load is invented. It follows a common California shape: a low overnight base (fridge, router, standby), a morning bump, a quiet middle and an evening peak from cooking, AC and TV. "Out during the day" totals about 16 kWh, close to the average PG&E home's 500 kWh a month. "Home all day" adds about 3 kWh of midday use.
  • Solar follows a clear July sky in San Jose. Output runs roughly 6:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. and peaks near 1 p.m. A balcony panel is rarely ideally tilted, so peak output is set at about 62% of the panel's rating before orientation and shade.
  • Prices are PG&E E-TOU-C summer rates from your 2026 bills: 52.24¢ per kWh from 4 to 9 p.m. and 39.94¢ at all other hours. Baseline credits and the daily base charge are left out, since solar doesn't change them.
  • Spilled power earns nothing. Plug-in systems aren't enrolled in net billing, so anything the home can't use at that moment flows to the grid uncredited. This is a sunny summer day, so a year-round average would be lower.
